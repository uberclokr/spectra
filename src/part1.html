How to read this chart

The scale is logarithmic — and it wraps

Each tenfold jump in frequency gets equal width, just like the NTIA wall chart. Zoomed out, the spectrum wraps onto up to three stacked rows — reading left to right, top to bottom — so every decade gets three times the width instead of being crushed into one strip. Zoom in and the rows merge back into one.

CAPITALS outrank lowercase

ITU convention: services in CAPITALS are primary — they get protection from interference. Lowercase services are secondary: welcome, but they must accept interference and cause none.

Bands stack when shared

Most bands host several services at once — each colored row in a column is one co-allocated service. Click any band for the full story; click a service in the legend to light up its territory across the whole spectrum.

Compare regulators

Switch the regulator menu and watch the map rearrange: Japan’s FM dial starts at 76 MHz, Europe’s longwave has no US counterpart, Australia’s CB lives at 476 MHz. Amber dashes mark every band that differs from the FCC table.

Controls

Scroll or pinch to zoom · drag to pan · double-click to dive · ←→ pan · +− zoom · 0 full view. The minimap below the chart always shows where you are; drag it to travel.

Rulers

Above the bands: ITU designators (VLF … EHF) and IEEE radar letters (L, S, C, X, Ku …). Below: frequency and wavelength — frequency × wavelength always equals the speed of light.

SPECTRA is an educational homage to the NTIA United States Frequency Allocations wall chart (2003 edition), rebuilt as a live instrument. Allocations are simplified from the FCC Online Table (47 CFR §2.106), Ofcom’s Frequency Allocation Table, Japan’s MIC assignment plans, the ACMA Australian table, and ISED Canada’s CTFA.

Band edges, service lists and notes are trimmed for teaching, not engineering — always consult the official tables before keying a transmitter. λ = c/f, and there is no cell service in a passive quiet band.
